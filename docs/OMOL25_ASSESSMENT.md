# OMol25 as the successor dataset — a measured assessment

*Written 2026-08-06, after the HOMO–LUMO gap audit. Every number attributed to
this project was produced by a script in `scripts/` and lands in `results/`;
every number attributed to OMol25 is cited to the paper (arXiv:2505.08762v2) or
its appendices. Where something is read off a dataset card rather than measured,
it says so.*

---

![The three results the assessment turns on](../figures/omol25_assessment.png)

## Verdict

**Take OMol25, but not for the reason that motivated the question, and not yet.**

The question was whether open-shell chemistry — transition-metal catalysts,
biological chromophores — plus labels with no mean-field analogue (ionization
energies, open-shell ground states) would give the quantum neuron something to
read that a classical model cannot. Three results, all new here, decide it:

1. **Ten second-quantization labels were built and tested on the existing 1000
   QH9 molecules. All ten fail** (§3). This includes labels that are *identically
   zero for any single-determinant state*: the correlation corrections to the
   IP, the EA and the singlet–triplet gap, the quasiparticle pole strength, and
   the unpaired-electron count. Quantum − classical runs from **−2.37 to +0.13
   points**. "No mean-field analogue" is therefore **not** a sufficient condition,
   and the label class the question proposes is, by itself, not the fix.

2. **The "these states are diagonal-dominated" result survives the obvious
   attack on it** (§2). The off-diagonal share of ρ is *not* basis-invariant —
   full Edmiston–Ruedenberg localization drives it from 0.058 to 0.929 — but
   that inflation is bookkeeping: a **zero-correlation single determinant goes to
   0.935 under the same rotation**, higher than the correlated state. Restricted
   to rotations that preserve the reference determinant, the share is invariant
   to 5·10⁻⁵ per molecule. The negative result is real, not an artifact.

3. **What actually gates the program is a single basis-invariant number, and QH9
   cannot supply it** (§4). The discriminant is the Head-Gordon count
   `N_unpaired = Σᵢ min(nᵢ, 2−nᵢ)`. Across all 1000 QH9 molecules it spans
   **0.0003 to 0.4815** (median 0.113) — **not one molecule above 0.5**. A single
   ethylene torsion coordinate spans **0.194 to 1.997**. QH9 in its entirety sits
   inside the first ~55° of one bond twist.

So the case for OMol25 is not "harder molecules, therefore more coherence to
learn from". It is narrower and stronger: **OMol25 is the only accessible source
of systems with no dominant reference determinant, which is the exact regime
where the diagonal/coherence split stops being decided in the diagonal's
favour** — demonstrated in §2 on twisted ethylene, where the same singlet/triplet
label moves from a screen ratio of 1.06 to 2451 as `N_unpaired` goes 0.19 → 2.00.

The "not yet" is because OMol25 **does not currently ship the Fock matrices**
(§1.2). That is the one hard blocker, and it has a workaround with a real price.

## 1. What OMol25 actually is

### 1.1 Scale and construction — verified

| | |
|---|---|
| Calculations | **140,641,161** in the full training split; ~83M unique systems |
| Level of theory | ωB97M-V / def2-TZVPD, ECPs for elements 1–83, **ORCA 6.0.0**, RI-J + COSX, DEFGRID3, `tight` |
| Elements | 83 |
| System size | 2–350 atoms, **mean 50** |
| Charge / spin | charge **−10 to +10**, spin multiplicity **1 to 11** |
| Licence | **CC BY 4.0** |
| Cost to produce | 6.6 billion CPU core-hours |

Domain shares, by number of atoms: electrolytes 30.8%, **metal complexes 18.9%**,
community 18.5%, biomolecules 17.7%, main-group 14.0%.

Splits: `All` 140,641,161 · `4M` 3,986,754 · `Neutral` 34,335,828 ·
val `Comp` 2,762,021 · test `Comp` 2,805,046, `M-Lig` 42,028, **`PDB-TM` 26,614**,
`Reactivity` 64,898, `COD` 84,807, `Anions` 16,488. **Splits are made by
composition (molecular formula)** — which is, for free, the composition-confound
control this project has been building by hand.

Two construction facts matter more than the headline numbers:

- **Metal complexes carry deliberate spin ladders.** Architector-generated
  complexes were run at the maximum reasonable spin state for the metal
  oxidation state, *and* at the lowest possible spin state, *and* — for
  multiplicity > 4 — at intermediate spin states. COD complexes: both highest and
  lowest reasonable spin for first-row TMs, low-spin only for 2nd/3rd row,
  high-spin only for lanthanides. **The same geometry at several spin states is
  exactly the fixed-composition, fixed-geometry, varying-correlation axis QH9
  lacks.**
- **Reactive geometries exist and are systematic.** Metal-complex reactivity was
  generated by AFIR reaction paths; main-group reactivity from
  reactant/TS/product triples; Transition-1X was recomputed in UKS. Bond-breaking
  geometries are the continuous version of the same axis.

### 1.2 What it stores — and the blocker

Appendix A.2 lists per-calculation outputs: total energy, forces, charge, spin,
atom/electron/ECP/basis counts, restricted-vs-unrestricted, SCF steps, VV10
energy, **⟨S²⟩ and its deviation from ideal**, integrated density, **HOMO energy
(α and β)**, **HOMO–LUMO gap (α and β)**, `fmax`, **Mulliken** charges and spins,
**Löwdin** charges and spins, **NBO** charges and spins (≤ 70 atoms), and ORCA
warnings.

> **The blocker.** §2.8 of the paper reads "the dataset includes ... orbital
> energies, Fock matrices, densities, and more as described in Appendix A.2",
> but A.2 is explicit that this is forward-looking: ORCA `.gbw` files and
> densities "will be made available **in the near future**", and multipole
> moments, **Fock matrices** and orbital energies "will be released in **future
> versions** of the dataset".
>
> Our Phase-1 pipeline consumes an AO-basis Fock matrix; that is precisely what
> lets it skip SCF entirely ([`INVARIANTS.md`](INVARIANTS.md) I2). **Today,
> OMol25 does not provide it.**

Three ways round it, in increasing cost:

| Route | What you get | Price |
|---|---|---|
| **OMol_CSH_58k** (arXiv:2510.00224) | 58k Hamiltonian matrices, 58 elements, systems up to 150 atoms, def2-TZVPD, derived from OMol25 | third-party; **closed-shell only**, so it excludes the open-shell metal complexes that motivated the whole idea |
| **Wait** for the Fock-matrix release | exactly what we want | unknown date; not a plan |
| **Run our own SCF** on OMol25 geometries (CC BY 4.0) | full control, open-shell included | the real cost (§7) — but note we would then choose the reference, which §2 shows is not a free choice |

### 1.3 Quality filters we would inherit

- ⟨S²⟩ **< 0.5 for open-shell metal-containing systems**, **< 1.1 otherwise**.
- Referenced energies ≤ ±150 eV; max per-atom force ≤ 50 eV/Å.
- α/β/total electron counts consistent with the integrated density.
- **Non-negative HOMO–LUMO gaps.**

And the reference construction: **all singlets containing TM or lanthanide
complexes, or where bonds are expected to break, were run in UKS with the HOMO
and LUMO rotated by 20° in the β space to break spin symmetry.** Non-singlets
are also UKS.

That last point is not a detail. A broken-symmetry UKS singlet is not a spin
eigenstate; it is a symmetry-broken single determinant standing in for a
multireference state. If we build an active space on those orbitals we inherit
the symmetry breaking, and §2 shows the reference determinant is exactly what
defines the diagonal/coherence split. **The reference we inherit determines the
answer we measure.** This has to be decided deliberately, not by default.

### 1.4 Stated limitations

From the paper's own outlook: no elements after bismuth; and coverage of
**lanthanide complexes, multimetallic structures**, and solvated protonated
organics and metal complexes is "relatively limited". Architector complexes are
**monometallic**. This matters for §5 candidate L1 (exchange coupling), which
needs two or more metal centres — the `PDB-TM` split (26,614 structures,
including 4Fe-4S clusters) is where those live, and it is small.

## 2. The methodological finding: is "coherence" basis-dependent? Yes — and it does not rescue the negative result

Every coherence number this project has quoted — the 6.7% off-diagonal share,
the R± screen ratios, the Z/ZZ-versus-XX/YY ablation — is computed in the
determinant basis built from **canonical, delocalized Kohn-Sham orbitals**. An
orthogonal rotation inside the active space changes no energy and no observable,
but redistributes weight between `diag(ρ)` and `offdiag(ρ)`. If the negative
result were an artifact of that choice, everything else would be moot.

**It is not, and the control is what proves it.**
`scripts/localized_basis_experiment.py`, all 1000 molecules, FCI ground states,
with the zero-correlation reference determinant carried through every rotation
by `fci.addons.transform_ci_for_orbital_rotation`:

| basis | correlated ground state | **zero-correlation control** |
|---|---|---|
| canonical (the pipeline's) | 0.0582 | 0.0000 |
| full Edmiston–Ruedenberg | 0.9293 | **0.9348** |
| **block ER** (occ–occ and virt–virt only) | **0.0580** | **0.0000** |

*(median `‖offdiag ρ‖²_F / ‖ρ‖²_F`; FCI energies invariant to 2.6·10⁻¹² Ha.)*

Full ER inflates the correlated state to 0.93 — and inflates a state with **no
correlation whatsoever** to 0.935, i.e. slightly *more*. That coherence is pure
bookkeeping from mixing occupied with virtual orbitals. Under the subgroup that
preserves the reference determinant, the share is unchanged: **max deviation
1.6·10⁻³, median 5.3·10⁻⁵** per molecule.

> **The rule this establishes.** The diagonal/coherence split is well defined
> *relative to a reference determinant*, and is invariant under every rotation
> that preserves it. A molecule offers genuine, non-bookkeeping coherence
> exactly to the extent that it **has no dominant reference**.

### 2.1 Where the split does flip: twisted ethylene

`scripts/basis_dependence_probe.py` — C₂H₄ torsion, B3LYP/def2-SVP orbitals,
CASCI(8,8), lowest S=0 against lowest S=1, SCF continuation along the scan,
FCI invariance gate at 2.3·10⁻¹⁴ Ha. Torsional barrier 98.5 kcal/mol; frontier
natural occupations 1.911/0.089 → 1.009/0.991.

| twist | `N_unpaired` | S–T gap | canonical: cos(diagS, diagT) / screen | localized: cos / screen |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0° | 0.194 | +4.247 eV | 0.0000 / 0.65 | 0.9229 / 4.7 |
| 40° | 0.260 | +2.820 eV | 0.0000 / 0.70 | 0.9422 / 5.7 |
| 70° | 0.650 | +0.724 eV | 0.0000 / 0.83 | 0.9879 / 11.0 |
| 85° | 1.520 | −0.024 eV | 0.0000 / 1.02 | 0.9999 / 92.1 |
| **90°** | **1.997** | −0.089 eV | 0.0000 / 1.06 | **1.0000 / 2451** |

*(screen = `‖offdiag(ρ_S − ρ_T)‖_F / ‖diag(ρ_S − ρ_T)‖_F`; QH9's median-split
HOMO–LUMO gap gives 0.1345 over 1000 molecules.)*

Read the two right-hand columns together. In the canonical basis the singlet and
triplet have **orthogonal populations at every angle** (cos = 0.0000): a model
reading only `diag(ρ)` separates them perfectly, everywhere. Rotate the frontier
pair onto the two carbons and at 90° the populations become **identical to four
decimals** while the states remain orthogonal — the entire label moves into the
sign of one coherence.

The rotation is occupied–virtual, i.e. exactly the kind §2 just called
bookkeeping. **It is legitimate here and only here**: at `N_unpaired` = 1.997 the
natural occupations are 1.009/0.991, so there is no reference determinant to
preserve and no privileged split to violate. That is the whole point, and it is
the precise condition OMol25 supplies and QH9 does not.

## 3. Ten second-quantization labels, and why all ten fail on QH9

`scripts/second_quantization_labels.py` solves other particle-number and spin
sectors of the *stored* active-space Hamiltonians — no QH9 access, no new SCF,
no run-file eigenvectors. Correctness gates: the independent FCI reproduces the
stored spectrum to **3.9·10⁻¹¹ Ha**, and the Slater's-rules determinant energies
match PySCF's CI energy evaluation to **exactly 0.0**. All 1000 molecules solved.

For each molecule, in the same active space and on the same orbitals: FCI ground
states of the neutral (4α,4β), cation (4α,3β), anion (5α,4β) and triplet
(5α,3β); plus the single-determinant energy of each sector's reference. The
differences are labels that **vanish identically for any single-determinant
state**.

`scripts/sq_label_screen.py`, held-out accuracy (%), 25 stratified 70/30 splits,
identical trainer throughout:

| label | vanishes for 1 det.? | composition | **diagonal (136)** | **full pool (248)** | coherence only (112) | **Q − C** | R² of diag |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| mean-field HOMO–LUMO gap *(reference)* | no | 84.9 | 94.8 | 93.5 | 53.0 | **−1.25** | 0.853 |
| correlation correction to the IP | **yes** | 81.7 | 82.1 | 82.3 | 52.2 | **+0.13** | 0.696 |
| correlation correction to the EA | **yes** | 71.8 | 74.3 | 71.9 | 50.9 | **−2.37** | 0.339 |
| correlation correction to the S–T gap | **yes** | 69.3 | 74.7 | 74.8 | **57.9** | **+0.12** | 0.469 |
| quasiparticle pole strength `Z` | **yes** | 81.6 | 83.4 | 82.1 | 55.9 | **−1.35** | 0.581 |
| unpaired-electron count (Head-Gordon) | **yes** | 84.3 | 95.4 | 94.6 | 51.7 | **−0.79** | 0.923 |
| double-excitation weight | **yes** | 84.3 | 95.4 | 94.6 | 51.7 | **−0.79** | 0.925 |
| active-space correlation energy | **yes** | 84.8 | 95.5 | 94.5 | 52.0 | **−1.00** | 0.875 |
| ionization energy (correlated) | no | 83.3 | 86.1 | 84.7 | 54.3 | **−1.45** | 0.848 |
| singlet–triplet gap (correlated) | no | 84.9 | 95.6 | 95.1 | 55.7 | **−0.45** | 0.946 |
| electron affinity (correlated) | no | 78.0 | 82.5 | 81.1 | 54.9 | **−1.45** | 0.710 |

The labels are not small: `IP_corr` has σ = 0.49 eV (5th–95th percentile −1.27
to +0.35 eV), `ST_corr` σ = 1.07 eV, and the pole strength runs down to
Z = 0.813 — a 19% quasiparticle-weight loss, a real photoemission satellite.
They are also not all composition proxies: `ST_corr` correlates with degree of
unsaturation at Spearman **+0.125** and `IP_corr` at **+0.328**, against +0.789
for `N_unpaired` and `w_double`. So this label class *does* partly escape the
confound that killed ⟨S²⟩ and the gap.

**They fail anyway, and §2 explains why.** In a reference-dominated basis, "how
correlated is this molecule" is *itself a diagonal readout* — it is the
population of the doubly-excited determinants. `w_double` and `N_unpaired` are
predicted identically (95.4 / 94.6 / 51.7 on all three feature sets) because in
this basis they are the same information. And the correlated IP, EA and S–T gap
are dominated by their own mean-field parts: r² = 0.906, 0.889, 0.815
respectively. What is left over is small, and small in the direction the
diagonal already covers.

### 3.1 Two escape hatches, both closed

- **Multireference stratification** (`scripts/multireference_stratification.py`):
  split the 1000 molecules into quartiles of `N_unpaired` and re-run the ablation
  inside each. **19 of 20 quartile×label deltas are negative**; the single
  positive (`ST_cas` Q3, +1.42) does not survive into Q4 (−5.00). No trend.
  *Caveat that matters:* the quartile medians span only 0.003 → 0.218, so this
  test is underpowered **by construction** — the whole dataset is in the
  single-reference regime. It is evidence that QH9 cannot answer the question,
  not evidence that the answer is no.
- **Sample complexity.** If coherence features were a better inductive bias they
  would pay at small *n*. They do the opposite: for `ST_corr`, Q − C is −3.56 at
  n=60, −5.16 at n=120, −3.22 at n=250, −0.16 at n=500, **+0.96** at n=700; for
  `IP_corr`, −8.53 → −0.60. The coherence block costs sample efficiency and the
  cost shrinks as data grows. There is no small-data advantage to harvest.

## 4. The discriminant, and where QH9 sits on it

Because the off-diagonal share is basis-relative (§2), the honest instrument is
a basis-invariant one. Use the natural-occupation spectrum, summarised by
`N_unpaired = Σᵢ min(nᵢ, 2−nᵢ)`: zero for any closed-shell determinant, 2 for a
perfect diradical, ≈ 2S for a high-spin centre.

QH9, 1000 molecules, CAS(8,8) ground states:

| statistic | min | p10 | median | p90 | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| weight on the reference determinant | 0.885 | 0.941 | **0.970** | 0.999 | 1.000 |
| `N_unpaired` | 0.0003 | 0.0026 | **0.113** | 0.226 | **0.4815** |
| quasiparticle weight `Z` | 0.813 | 0.920 | 0.962 | 0.995 | 0.999 |
| off-diagonal share (canonical) | 0.0007 | 0.0026 | 0.058 | 0.115 | 0.217 |

**Zero molecules** have reference weight below 0.88, and **zero** have
`N_unpaired` above 0.5. On the ethylene scale, QH9's most multireference molecule
sits between 50° and 60° of twist, and QH9's *median* (0.113) is **less
multireference than planar ethylene** (0.194).

That is the whole finding in one line: **QH9 does not contain the regime the
program requires, and the shortfall is a factor of 4–20 in the invariant, not a
few percent.**

## 5. Label catalogue for OMol25, ranked

The admission criterion follows from §2 and §4, and it applies to the *system*
before it applies to the label:

> **A system is admissible iff it has no dominant reference determinant** —
> operationally `N_unpaired ≳ 1`, or equivalently at least one natural occupation
> in [0.5, 1.5]. **A label is admissible iff two admissible systems can share the
> same determinant populations and differ in the label.**

QH9 admits **0 of 1000** molecules. Everything below is scored against that bar.

### L1 — Magnetic exchange coupling `J` in polynuclear complexes · **strongest, supply-limited**

Two metal centres, identical local d-occupations, differing only in whether the
local moments are ferro- or antiferromagnetically coupled. The *populations* are
the same by construction; only the inter-centre coherence differs. This is the
§2 ethylene construction with chemistry attached, and `J` spans orders of
magnitude across ligand bridges at near-fixed composition.

- **Admissible:** yes, maximally — 4Fe-4S clusters have `N_unpaired` ≈ 8–10.
- **Supply:** the constraint. OMol25's Architector complexes are **monometallic**;
  the paper lists multimetallic coverage as "relatively limited". The realistic
  source is the `PDB-TM` test split (26,614 structures, incl. 4Fe-4S) plus COD.
- **Catch:** OMol25's broken-symmetry UKS singlets are exactly the
  antiferromagnetic states, and ⟨S²⟩ < 0.5 filtering for metal systems may have
  *removed* the most strongly coupled cases. Verify before planning.

### L2 — Spin-state splitting on the OMol25 spin ladder · **best supply, ready-made**

The dataset was built for this: 1000 electrolytes + 3000 Architector complexes +
5000 COD complexes, each with **exactly one open-shell metal and at least two
viable spin states**, with the **full ladder of viable spin states simulated at
one geometry**.

- **Admissible:** yes; high-spin first-row TM centres give `N_unpaired` ≈ 4.
- **Why it is the right target:** it is OMol25's own evaluation task, and the
  paper reports that it **resists model scaling** — best baseline (UMA-M-1.1)
  7.73 kcal/mol ΔE MAE against 1.38 kcal/mol on plain energies, and larger models
  improve IE/EA by ~50% but spin gaps by **only ~25%**, "suggesting that more
  than just model scaling will be required".
- **Honesty check, and it is important:** that failure is of models reading
  *(Z, R, charge, spin)*. Our neuron reads ρ, which is obtained by solving the
  electronic structure. Beating an MLIP is not the claim and would not be
  meaningful. The claim must remain the internal one: **full pool vs diagonal
  pool, same ρ, same trainer.**

### L3 — Ionization energy of an open-shell centre · **the question as asked, downgraded**

Measured on QH9: the correlation correction to the IP is real (σ = 0.49 eV) and
only weakly composition-confounded (Spearman +0.328 vs DoU) — but Q − C = +0.13,
i.e. nothing (§3). It is downgraded not because it is a bad quantity but because
we have now tested it. On OMol25 it becomes interesting again **only** through
admissibility: ionizing an open-shell metal centre changes the coupling pattern,
not just an occupation. Test it as `IP(high-spin) − IP(low-spin)` on the same
complex rather than as a bare IP.

### L4 — Position along an AFIR reaction path · **continuous, fixed composition**

`N_unpaired` sweeps continuously from ~0 to ~2 along a bond-breaking coordinate
with composition *exactly* constant — the ethylene scan generalised, and OMol25
generates these systematically for both metal complexes and main-group
reactivity. Label on the correlation content, or on which side of the transition
state a structure lies. Perfectly controlled; the risk is that geometry alone
predicts it, so it needs a geometry-descriptor baseline as well as a composition
one.

### L5 — Conformer-resolved anything · **the cheapest confound control available**

OMol25 ships conformers, and its splits are already by composition. Any label
evaluated *within* a conformer family has the composition confound eliminated by
construction rather than by residualization. Use this as a control layer on L1–L4
rather than as a label in itself.

### Explicitly rejected

- **Bare HOMO–LUMO gap** — one-body observable; closed 2026-08-06.
- **Correlation energy, `w_double`, `N_unpaired` as targets** — in a
  reference-dominated basis these *are* diagonal readouts (R² = 0.92–0.93, §3),
  and Spearman +0.79 against degree of unsaturation.
- **Total energies / forces** — OMol25's own MLIPs reach 1.38 kcal/mol; there is
  no room and no mechanism.

## 6. What it would take to use OMol25 properly

The pipeline is `S_z = 0`, closed-shell, single-Fock-matrix, frontier-window
throughout. Each item below is a real change, ordered by dependency.

**A. Data access.**
1. `fairchem`/ASE reader for the OMol25 record format; a loader analogous to
   `qthermal/loader.py` for `(Z, R, charge, spin)` plus the stored scalars.
2. A subset selector. 140M calculations is not a corpus we ingest; we take
   thousands. Selection is by the §5 admission criterion, and the only cheap
   proxies available *before* computing anything are the shipped ⟨S²⟩,
   α/β HOMO–LUMO gaps, and Mulliken/Löwdin **spin populations** — the last is the
   practical pre-filter for "is there an open-shell centre".

**B. The reference problem — decide it explicitly.**
3. No Fock matrices today (§1.2), so we run SCF. Choosing ROKS/UKS/broken-symmetry
   *is choosing the diagonal/coherence split* (§2). Recommendation: **CASSCF
   orbitals, not SCF orbitals**, for admissible systems — orbital-optimized, and
   the natural occupations that define admissibility come out of the same
   calculation. This breaks the "no SCF" invariant deliberately and for a stated
   reason; it does not amend I2, which governs the QH9 path.
4. Spin contamination has to be carried as a diagnostic, not filtered silently.

**C. Open-shell support in `qthermal`.**
5. `active_space.py:88` asserts `nelecas % 2 == 0 and nalpha == nbeta`. Generalise
   to arbitrary `(nα, nβ)`. `encode.py` is **already** parameterised on
   `(ncas, nalpha, nbeta)` and needs no change.
6. `hamiltonian.make_injected_rhf` assumes RHF with `mo_occ = 2`. Needs a UHF/ROHF
   path, or the CASSCF route in B3 makes it moot.
7. **Thermal ensembles across spin sectors.** At fixed particle number and no
   field, the physical Gibbs state spans all `S_z`, not one sector. `thermal.py`
   and `diagonalize.py` currently truncate within one sector. For spin-state
   labels this is not cosmetic — it is the label. Decide: per-sector states
   (cleaner, what L2 wants) or a genuine multi-sector ensemble (more physical,
   more work).
8. The 248-term extended-Heisenberg basis assumes real states and `S_z`
   conservation and drops cross-spin XX/YY. Both survive fixed `S_z ≠ 0`; the
   basis size and the `⟨XX⟩ = ⟨YY⟩` degeneracy do not, and the ablation's
   dimension bookkeeping must be recomputed.

**D. Active-space selection — the substantive scientific change.**
9. The frontier window (`active_space.py`) is wrong for a metal complex: it will
   take ligand π orbitals and miss the d shell. Needs **AVAS** (`pyscf.mcscf.avas`)
   or an atomic-valence selection targeting the metal d manifold plus the ligand
   frontier set. This is the single highest-risk item — a bad active space
   produces confident wrong numbers, and §4's `N_unpaired` is computed *inside*
   the active space, so a bad window silently reports a system as inadmissible.
10. Basis: def2-TZVPD has diffuse functions and near-linear-dependence problems
    in PySCF; needs an overlap-eigenvalue threshold and a canonical-orthogonalization
    path.

**E. Instrumentation.**
11. Report `N_unpaired` and reference weight for every system, as first-class
    outputs. They are the admission criterion.
12. Screen the label's **residual** against an out-of-fold `diag(ρ)` model, never
    the raw label ([`INVARIANTS.md`](INVARIANTS.md) I16).
13. Carry the zero-correlation determinant control (§2) in any future coherence
    measurement. It is four lines and it is the difference between a result and
    an artifact.

## 7. Cost, and where it actually lands

Not where people expect. **The CI dimension is set by the active space, not by
the molecule:**

| active space | determinant dimension (`S_z = 0`) | vs CAS(8,8) |
|---|---|---|
| CAS(8,8) | 4,900 | 1× |
| CAS(10,10) | 63,504 | 13× |
| CAS(12,12) | 853,776 | 174× |
| CAS(14,14) | 11,778,624 | 2,400× |

A first-row TM complex needs the metal d shell (5 orbitals) plus the ligand
frontier set: **CAS(10,10) is a floor, CAS(12,12) realistic**. CAS(8,8) is not
defensible there, so the 13–174× is not optional.

What grows with the *dataset* rather than the active space:

- **Integral transform.** def2-SVP on 9 heavy atoms is ~120 AOs; def2-TZVPD on a
  100-atom complex is a few thousand. The AO→MO transform is the new dominant
  cost, and it is per-molecule, not per-state.
- **SCF/CASSCF**, which QH9 gave us for free.
- **Storage.** The 1000-molecule CAS(8,8) kT = 0.1 run is **45 GB**. At
  CAS(10,10) the eigenvector block alone is 13× longer per state, and the thermal
  window at fixed kT holds more states. A 1000-system CAS(10,10) campaign is a
  multi-TB object unless we stop storing eigenvectors and store only the derived
  features — which, given §3, is what we should do.

Measured reference points from this project: `--solver iterative` runs
**4.5–6 min/molecule at CAS(10,10), kT = 0.025**; at kT = 0.1 the Krylov window
is wider and escalation may cap out ([`OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`](OPEN_QUESTIONS.md) Q3,
Q8). Add CASSCF and a TZVPD transform and a few minutes per system becomes tens.

**A 2,000-system pilot at CAS(10,10) is therefore a ~1–2 core-week job plus
storage discipline — affordable. A 100,000-system campaign is not, and nothing in
§3 suggests we need one.**

There is also a live warning from our own data: CAS(8,8) already truncates the
*thermal* states of high-DoU QH9 molecules (top-decile median edge slack 0.167,
Q3). Moving to more correlated chemistry without widening the window means
representing the interesting systems worst.

## 8. Likelihood of advantage

Decomposed into conditions that must all hold, with what is measured and what is
assumed:

| # | Condition | Status | P |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | OMol25 supplies systems with `N_unpaired ≳ 1` in useful numbers | Construction is explicit (spin ladders, 9,000 spin-gap systems, AFIR paths). Not verified by us — no Fock matrices, no calculations run. | **0.9** |
| 2 | In that regime, a label exists whose populations coincide across classes | Demonstrated on real chemistry: ethylene 90°, cos(diag) = 1.0000, screen 1.06 → 2451, FCI-invariant. Single system, not a dataset. | **0.75** |
| 3 | That property survives *at the dataset level*, i.e. the coherence is not redundant with `diag(ρ)` across many systems | **Never yet observed in this project.** Three label families have now failed exactly here (⟨S²⟩/c, the gap, ten second-quantization labels). No positive instance at n > 1. | **0.35** |
| 4 | It also escapes composition and geometry descriptors | OMol25 splits by composition and supplies conformers/spin ladders at fixed geometry, so this is controllable by construction — the best-supported link in the chain. | **0.8** |
| 5 | The active space can be chosen well enough that 1–4 are not destroyed | Highest-risk engineering item (§6 D). No experience with TM active spaces in this codebase. | **0.6** |
| 6 | The trained model actually realizes it | The hybrid network reaches 93.0% on an exactly-representable control against a 77.0% ablation, so the machinery works when the signal is there ([`RESEARCH_LOG.md`](RESEARCH_LOG.md) 2026-08-06). | **0.85** |

Multiplying: **≈ 0.10**, with condition 3 carrying almost all the risk.

Read that number carefully. It is *not* "OMol25 probably will not work". It is
**"the only untested link is the one that has failed three times, and OMol25 is
the first dataset that can test it at all."** QH9 could never have tested it: with
zero admissible systems, conditions 2 and 3 were unreachable in principle, which
means every negative result to date is uninformative about them.

The calibration to hold on to: on the *same* 1000 states, the same pool and the
same trainer, a synthetic purely off-diagonal label separates the pools by
**+42.0 points**. The instrument has never been the problem. The regime has.

## 9. The decisive test, staged

Each stage is cheap, and each can kill the program before the next is funded.

**Stage 0 — free, days.** Does OMol25 actually contain what §5 needs? Download
metadata only. Count systems with (a) ≥ 2 spin states at one geometry, (b) a
Mulliken/Löwdin spin population > 0.5 on a metal centre, (c) ⟨S²⟩ deviation
indicating broken symmetry. **Kill criterion:** fewer than ~2,000 usable
multi-spin-state systems after the ⟨S²⟩ < 0.5 filter.

**Stage 1 — ~1 core-week.** Take 200 spin-ladder complexes. CASSCF(10,10) with
AVAS. Report `N_unpaired` and reference weight. **Kill criterion:** median
`N_unpaired` < 1.0, i.e. the active space or the filtering has put us back in
QH9's regime.

**Stage 2 — hours after Stage 1.** On those 200, compute the L2 spin-gap label
and run the **residual** screen (I16) and the zero-correlation control (§2).
**Kill criterion:** off-diagonal features explain < 5% of the variance of the
spin-gap residual after an out-of-fold `diag(ρ)` model. This is the condition-3
test, and it is the whole question.

**Stage 3 — only if Stage 2 passes.** Scale to ~2,000 systems and run the full
ablation with the hybrid network, against composition, geometry, and diagonal
baselines.

Stage 2 is the experiment this project has been unable to run for its entire
history. Getting to it is the point of adopting OMol25.
